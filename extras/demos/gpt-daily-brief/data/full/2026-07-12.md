## 2. CS / AI developments

- **ZipDepth tries to move foundation-model depth estimation onto actual devices.** The new 6.1-million-parameter model combines a reparameterizable encoder–decoder with large-scale distillation from a depth foundation model over multiple domains. The authors report real-time operation from server GPUs down to power-constrained hardware, with the best accuracy–efficiency trade-off among lightweight methods across five zero-shot benchmarks and performance approaching models roughly 50 times larger. The interesting part is not merely compression: multi-domain distillation is being used to prevent a small model from silently becoming a single-domain specialist.
- **Whareformer treats long-video memory as an entity database, not a longer context window.** For egocentric video, it maintains updateable object tracks containing evolving appearance and 3D location, then assigns new observations to those tracks with a feed-forward module and a dedicated “new track” token. The authors trained on only 56 videos and report state-of-the-art results on 260 long videos across three datasets. The broader systems lesson is useful: persistent world state may be cheaper and more controllable than repeatedly asking attention to rediscover identity from raw history.

Both are recent preprints/ECCV 2026 papers; the quantitative claims are the authors’ reported results, not independent replications.

## 3. Technical / research idea — invertible Bloom lookup tables

Suppose two replicas each contain millions of object IDs, but differ by only a few thousand. Sending either complete set is wasteful; a normal Bloom filter can reveal probable membership but cannot enumerate the missing objects.

An **invertible Bloom lookup table** (IBLT) stores, in each hashed cell, a signed item count, the XOR of item keys and the XOR of a checksum. Each replica builds the same-sized table, and one subtracts the other cell by cell. A cell with count `+1` or `-1` and a matching checksum reveals one item in the symmetric difference. Removing that item from its other hashed cells exposes more singletons, producing a peeling decoder analogous to sparse-graph error correction.

The communication cost can therefore scale with the expected **difference** *d*, rather than the total set size *n*. If the difference is larger than the table was provisioned for, peeling fails and the parties retry with a larger sketch. That failure mode is a feature: the sketch is compact because it prices uncertainty about divergence explicitly.

## 4. Non-CS science developments

- **SpudCell is a serious integration result, but not autonomous synthetic life.** A University of Minnesota team reports bottom-up vesicles made from chemically specified components that can grow, copy synthetic DNA and divide. The central advance is closing several previously separate modules into a repeatable cell cycle.
- **Its dependency graph matters more than the headline.** The vesicles live in an ATP-rich medium and fuse with feeder liposomes that supply enzymes and ribosomes; they cannot generate their own energy, protein-making machinery or waste-management system, and they deteriorate after a few generations. The work is a preprint. Its present significance is as a controllable chassis for studying which coupled processes are sufficient for life-like dynamics—not that researchers have crossed an agreed boundary into “creating life.”

## 6. Signal vs noise

**Noise:** treating a presidential announcement, summit statement or signed memorandum as the completed policy.

**Signal:** the implementation chain. Congress can block an arms transfer; banks can refuse sanctioned transactions; insurers and crews can keep ships out of a formally “open” strait; local armed actors can make reconstruction contracts unusable. Modern state power is often distributed across legal, financial, logistical and commercial veto points. The announcement changes bargaining expectations; the chain determines whether anything happens.

## 7. Marxist quote / commentary

In the *Grundrisse*, Marx writes: **“Capital by its nature drives beyond every spatial barrier.”**

The notebooks were composed in 1857–58, when railways, steam shipping and the telegraph were rapidly reorganizing the world market. Marx’s surrounding argument is not that geography disappears. Capital must reduce circulation time because money tied up in transit cannot immediately begin another cycle of accumulation; it therefore builds transport and communication systems to produce what he calls the “annihilation of space by time.”

Hormuz reveals the contradiction. Global accumulation constructs extraordinary systems for moving commodities quickly, then concentrates them through ports, canals, pipelines and straits whose interruption reasserts geography with exceptional force. Capital tries to overcome spatial barriers while continually creating infrastructures whose bottlenecks become strategic power.

## 8. Two philosophy / political fragments

- **Thomas Schelling — compellence is structurally harder than deterrence.** Deterrence tells an adversary not to begin an action and can succeed without any visible event. Compellence demands that the adversary stop, withdraw or perform something observable, usually under a deadline while punishment is already being applied. This creates disputes over what counts as compliance, whose move comes next and whether a pause signals concession or preparation. “Reopen the strait” is therefore a more escalation-prone demand than “do not close it.”
- **Simone Weil — force dehumanizes through mechanism.** In *The Iliad, or the Poem of Force*, written in 1939, she defines force as **“that which makes a thing of whoever is subjected to it.”** The immediate meaning is the corpse, but her larger point includes the living person reduced to obedience, hunger, fear or calculation. Weil also argues that the apparent possessor of force becomes intoxicated and governed by it. Strategy describes actors using violence instrumentally; she asks when the instrument has begun to organize the actors.

## 9. French language note — *en avoir pour*

**En avoir pour + duration/amount** means that a task, journey or purchase will take a certain amount of time or cost a certain amount.

- *J’en ai pour dix minutes.* = I’ll be ten minutes / it will take me ten minutes.
- *T’en as pour longtemps ?* = Are you going to be long?
- *On en a eu pour 42 euros.* = It came to €42.

The `en` refers loosely to the current business or situation. In speech, *tu en as* normally contracts to *t’en as*. Compare *j’arrive dans dix minutes* (I will arrive in ten minutes) with *j’en ai pour dix minutes* (the thing I am doing will take ten minutes).

## 11. Unrelated fresh fact — some dunes can sing

A small number of dry sand dunes produce a sustained booming note when an avalanche runs down their slip face. The sound can last for minutes and has a relatively definite pitch; it is generated by the moving grains, not simply by wind blowing across the dune.

Experiments indicate that similarly sized grains can synchronize their motion in the flowing layer, allowing millions of weak grain interactions to add into a coherent acoustic signal. Researchers still dispute exactly how the dune’s surface and subsurface layers select and amplify the frequency, so “resonance” is not a settled single mechanism. Singing dunes occur in places including the Sahara, China and the western United States, and descriptions go back at least to medieval travel accounts. They matter scientifically because a disordered granular material can spontaneously behave like a macroscopic oscillator.

## 12. Puzzle / dilemma — the teletransporter

A machine scans your body and brain, destroys the original, and reconstructs an atom-for-atom version on Mars with all your memories, dispositions and sense of continuity.

Do you survive?

Now change one detail: the destruction mechanism fails. You remain on Earth while the Mars version wakes up sincerely remembering entering the machine. If the first process counted as survival, why does the existence of the Earth version change the answer? If both are you, can personal identity branch? If only one is you, what non-arbitrary fact selects it?
