## 2. CS / AI developments

- **Video QA is being forced to show its evidence.** *Evidence-Backed Video Question Answering* requires a model to produce not only a semantic answer, but also the relevant time segments and dense tracked object masks across those segments. That changes grounding from a post-hoc visualization into an explicit output and evaluation target. Its ST-Evidence benchmark is useful because ordinary answer accuracy can remain high while spatial-temporal evidence is poor; scaling alone did not close that gap. A 160,000-example instruction set substantially improved tracked-mask quality in a size-matched 7B model. The remaining concern is whether models learn the annotation conventions rather than genuinely better evidence selection. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.11862)
- **An open 4B video model puts temporal compression before the language model.** *VideoChat3* combines an I3D-style vision transformer with adaptive frame resolution for streaming video, allocating representation capacity according to temporal and visual needs rather than treating every frame uniformly. The practical point is architectural: long-video efficiency depends on temporal mixing and resolution decisions before visual tokens reach the LLM. The release is also unusually open about model, data and training. Benchmark breadth, however, is not a substitute for measured deployment latency, peak memory and behaviour under genuinely unbounded streams. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.14935)

## 3. Technical / research idea — semi-naive Datalog evaluation

A recursive Datalog program is commonly evaluated to a fixed point: derive facts, add them to the database, and repeat until no new facts appear. The naive method reruns each rule over **all** known tuples every round, repeatedly rediscovering the same consequences.

**Semi-naive evaluation** keeps a delta relation, Δ, containing only facts added in the previous round. Each recursive rule is evaluated through variants in which at least one recursive input comes from Δ. Newly produced facts are deduplicated against the accumulated relation, then become the next delta. In a reachability query, this means extending only the newly discovered frontier rather than recomputing every path from the beginning.

The gain is not just a constant factor: recursive joins can otherwise redo exponentially or polynomially growing amounts of old work. The complication is semantic and engineering bookkeeping—mutually recursive predicates require several delta variants, while negation and aggregation need restrictions or more careful fixed-point semantics. The same idea appears in deductive databases, static analysis and incremental computation. [POPL 2020](https://popl20.sigplan.org/details/POPL-2020-Research-Papers/3/Semina-ve-Evaluation-for-a-Higher-Order-Functional-Language)

## 4. Non-CS science

- **Mechanical instability can be designed as fracture protection rather than treated as failure.** A new *Nature* study used pseudoplastic architected materials whose unit cells snap through under load. By controlling the size of the inelastic zone around a crack, the researchers shifted the material from intrinsic fracture—where resistance is mostly set at the crack tip—to extrinsic toughening, where a larger surrounding region dissipates energy and impedes propagation.
- Experiments and simulations reported up to an order-of-magnitude increase in fracture energy. The broader engineering idea is programmable failure: geometry can determine where elastic energy is released and how cracks are arrested without relying only on a tougher base material. The result is still demonstrated in architected model systems; fatigue, defect sensitivity, manufacturability and scaling to structural components remain the real tests. [Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-10804-0)

## 5. Geopolitics — the S-400 as a four-state bargaining problem

Turkey is reportedly discussing transferring its Russian-made S-400 air-defence system to the UAE, a move intended to remove a central U.S. objection to Ankara's return to the F-35 programme. This is not a completed sale. Russia says it remains in contact with Turkey over the system, while the U.S. concern has been that operating the S-400 alongside the F-35 could expose sensitive aircraft signatures and tactics. [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/kremlin-says-russia-is-touch-with-turkey-fate-russian-s-400-missiles-2026-07-10/) · [WSJ](https://www.wsj.com/world/turkey-aims-to-offload-russian-air-defense-system-as-it-seeks-to-buy-u-s-f-35s-70d05162)

The interesting structure is a **stacked-veto transaction**. Russia may have contractual or political leverage over re-export. Washington would have to judge that the Turkish security problem was materially removed, decide how to handle CAATSA sanctions, and then navigate executive, congressional and procurement decisions around the F-35. The UAE could also inherit its own co-location problem if it still wants advanced U.S. aircraft. The S-400 is therefore no longer merely military hardware; it is a bargaining asset whose disposal requires four states to accept different risks. Moving it can relocate the incompatibility without automatically resolving it.

## 7. Marxist fragment

> “force is only the means, and that the aim, on the contrary, is economic advantage.”

— Friedrich Engels, *Anti-Dühring*, Part II, Chapter IV, English translation published by Progress Publishers. [Primary text](https://www.marxists.org/archive/marx/works/1877/anti-duhring/ch14.htm)

Engels is arguing against explanations that make political force self-sufficient. Coercion is real, but armies, weapons and state power must be materially reproduced and are generally deployed toward control of property, revenue, labour or strategic advantage. This is not a claim that ideology and political autonomy are illusions. It is a demand to ask what material relation a coercive arrangement secures—and who pays to sustain it. The S-400 dispute is illustrative: military systems function simultaneously as defence assets, industrial contracts, sanctions triggers and bargaining instruments.

## 8. Two philosophy / political fragments

### Hobbes — promises require an enforcement structure

> “Covenants, without the Sword, are but Words, and of no strength to secure a man at all.”

— Thomas Hobbes, *Leviathan*, Chapter XVII. [Primary text](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/3207/3207-h/3207-h.htm)

Hobbes's point is not simply that might creates legitimacy. A promise changes behaviour only when parties expect defection to be detected and sanctioned. Domestic institutions concentrate that enforcement; international agreements usually distribute it among states with unequal capabilities, which makes verification, retaliation and credible commitment part of the agreement rather than external details.

### Kant — peace is an institution, not a pause

> “A state of peace among men who live side by side is not the natural state.”

— Immanuel Kant, *Perpetual Peace*, Second Section, preamble to the definitive articles, M. Campbell Smith translation. [Primary text](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/50922/pg50922-images.html)

Kant agrees with Hobbes that peace must be produced, but rejects the conclusion that a single world sovereign is the only route. His alternative is a juridical federation among states. The useful test for any “rules-based order” is consequently institutional: does it alter incentives, verification and the cost of violation, or does it merely name a preferred norm?

## 9. French note — *quitte à + infinitif*

**Quitte à** marks a cost or consequence that the speaker accepts: “even if it means…” It also commonly pairs with **autant** to mean “since we are doing this anyway, we may as well…”

- *Quitte à venir jusqu'ici, autant rester pour le concert.* — Since we came all this way, we might as well stay for the concert.
- *Je vais vérifier moi-même, quitte à perdre dix minutes.* — I'll check myself, even if it costs ten minutes.

It is not quite the same as *au risque de*: *quitte à* presents the consequence as consciously accepted, while *au risque de* foregrounds an unwanted possibility.

## 11. Unrelated fresh fact — the ocean's nightly false floor

Early sonar operators sometimes detected what looked like a seabed that rose toward the surface at night and sank again by day. It was the **deep scattering layer**: dense fish, squid and zooplankton reflecting sound strongly enough to produce a false bottom on sonar. [NOAA](https://oceanexplorer.noaa.gov/ocean-fact/dsl/)

The movement is **diel vertical migration**. Many organisms remain hundreds of metres deep during daylight to reduce predation, then rise after dark to feed near the surface. By biomass and number of animals, it is often described as the planet's largest organized migration. It also forms an active carbon pump: organisms consume carbon fixed near the surface and transport part of it downward through respiration, waste and predation. [NOAA Ocean Exploration](https://oceanexplorer.noaa.gov/expedition-feature/technology-development-partnerships-21scattering-layer-features-scattering-layer/) · [WHOI](https://www.whoi.edu/?visual_whoi_item=otz-pumps)

## 12. Puzzle — the 1-or-19-minute bus

A bus route has independent gaps of either **1 minute** or **19 minutes**, each occurring with probability **1/2**. The operator therefore reports a mean interval of **10 minutes**.

You arrive at the stop at a uniformly random time in the long-run process, with no timetable and no information about when the previous bus left.

**What is your expected waiting time?** Is it 5 minutes, as “half the average interval” suggests? Which intervals are you more likely to arrive inside, and what sampling assumption changes the answer? [Background on the inspection paradox](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.05883)
