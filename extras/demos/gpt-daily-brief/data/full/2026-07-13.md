## 2. CS / AI developments

- **On-device VLM energy may be dominated by language decoding, not vision.** A new profiling study tested five VLMs across three architecture families, four image resolutions and both an RTX 3070 and Jetson Orin NX. The authors report that each generated token costs **11–39 times** as much wall-clock time as an input token; deleting every visual token saved at most 10% of total energy in fixed-output tests, while controlling answer length saved as much as 97%. The practical implication is sharp: optimizing a camera agent around visual-token pruning can target the wrong bottleneck if it still produces verbose autoregressive output.
- **A new pretraining study treats rendered documents as the knowledge substrate rather than text-extraction inputs.** Instead of OCR-converting papers and web pages into plain text, the models learn directly from page images containing typography, equations, figures and layout. Across several backbones and benchmarks, the authors report that visual pretraining on the same underlying corpora consistently outperformed text-only pretraining. The architectural question is more interesting than the aggregate result: a page is not merely a bag of recognized characters, because spatial grouping and notation carry information that a linear text serialization may erase.

Both are recent preprints; the quantitative results are author-reported and not independent replications.

## 3. Technical / research idea — Elias–Fano encoding

Suppose an inverted index stores a sorted posting list of `n` document IDs drawn from a universe of size `U`. Storing every ID in `log₂ U` bits ignores the fact that the sequence is monotone. Delta coding exploits the gaps, but random access usually requires decoding earlier gaps.

**Elias–Fano encoding** splits every integer into low and high bits. Roughly `log₂(U/n)` low bits are stored verbatim. The nondecreasing high parts are represented by a unary-style bitvector: place the `i`th one at position `high[i] + i`. A `select` operation finds that one, subtracting `i` recovers the high part, and concatenating the stored low bits reconstructs the value.

The result uses about `n log₂(U/n) + O(n)` bits—close to the information-theoretic bound for sparse sorted sets—while retaining fast indexed access and skipping. This is why Elias–Fano and partitioned variants fit search-engine posting lists, graph adjacency lists and monotone offsets: the representation compresses **order itself**, rather than merely applying a generic codec afterward.

## 4. Non-CS science developments

- **Researchers are programming auxetic behaviour directly into machine-knitted fabric.** A new materials paper combines rib and garter patches in checkerboard cells that relax into corrugations. When stretched, the folds first unfold, allowing the textile to widen rather than narrow: a tunable negative Poisson ratio produced by geometry rather than an exotic material.
- **The same knitting pass can embed a strain sensor.** Conductive yarn is partially plated into the textile to form a capacitor, avoiding a separate sensor-attachment step. Changing the cell geometry trades sensing range against sensitivity. The credible near-term value is not “smart clothing” as a slogan, but scalable soft structures whose deformation and measurement behaviour are co-designed at stitch level.

## 6. Signal vs noise

**Noise:** counting air-defence launchers, summit pledges or headline euros as if they were interchangeable units of military capability.

**Signal:** the complete engagement chain—radar coverage, trained crews, command integration, missiles available per launcher, reload time, production lead time and the target mix. Cheap drones, cruise missiles and ballistic missiles impose different interception economics. A state can own an advanced battery yet be unable to spend a scarce multi-million-euro interceptor on every incoming object. Capability is therefore a queueing and inventory problem as much as a procurement total.

## 7. Marxist quote / commentary

In *Capital*, Volume I, Marx writes: **“The public debt becomes one of the most powerful levers of primitive accumulation.”**

The passage concerns the rise of European national-debt systems alongside colonial trade, taxation, protectionism and early central banking. Marx’s point is not simply that government borrowing is intrinsically harmful. Public debt converts a claim on future tax revenue into a liquid, tradable asset; money lent to the state continues circulating as bonds, while financiers and banks gain fees, interest and institutional privileges.

That helps explain why fiscal crises are class conflicts over both sides of the state balance sheet. The debt may have financed war, infrastructure, industrial support or social expenditure, but adjustment is rarely imposed symmetrically on bondholders, firms, wage-earners and service users. “There is no money” is therefore not an accounting conclusion by itself; it is a claim about which obligations are treated as inviolable and which are politically revisable.

## 8. Two philosophy / political fragments

- **John Dewey — publics form around indirect consequences.** In *The Public and Its Problems* (1927), Dewey defines a public as those affected by the indirect consequences of transactions strongly enough that those consequences require organized care. This avoids treating “the public” as a pre-existing national will. Energy shocks, missile production, insurance rules or platform algorithms create different overlapping publics because their effects spill beyond the parties making the initial decision. The democratic problem is often that the affected group exists materially before it can identify itself politically.
- **Carl Schmitt — the exception reveals who can suspend the normal rule.** *Political Theology* opens with the claim: **“Sovereign is he who decides on the exception.”** The analytical force is that emergencies expose where final authority actually lies when rules cannot determine their own application. The danger is equally central: whoever declares the emergency can also manufacture the conditions for escaping ordinary constraint. Schmitt treats decisive authority as the foundation of order; constitutional politics has to answer how an emergency power can remain temporary when the sovereign is also judging whether the emergency continues.

## 9. French language note — *tant qu’à faire*

**Tant qu’à faire** means roughly “while we’re at it,” “since we’re doing it anyway,” or “we might as well.” It introduces an additional action justified by the effort or situation already underway.

- *Tant qu’à être dans le quartier, on peut passer voir l’expo.*  
  Since we’ll be in the area anyway, we might as well see the exhibition.
- *Tant qu’à attendre une heure, autant aller manger quelque chose.*  
  If we’re going to wait an hour anyway, we may as well get something to eat.
- *Tant qu’à faire, prends-en deux.*  
  While you’re at it, take two.

Do not confuse it with **tant que**, meaning “as long as” or “while”: *tant que le métro roule* = as long as the metro is running. In speech, *tant qu’à faire, autant…* is a very common pairing.

## 11. Unrelated fresh fact — the Andes developed natural freeze-drying

**Chuño** is a preserved potato made traditionally by Quechua and Aymara communities on the high Andean Altiplano. Potatoes are exposed to freezing nights and intense daytime sun, then trampled to expel water and remove skins before further drying. The freeze–thaw cycles rupture cells, while the final low moisture content sharply slows microbial and enzymatic spoilage.

The method is adapted to the large daily temperature swings above roughly 3,800 metres in Peru and Bolivia. Chuño can remain usable for years or even decades, is much lighter to transport than fresh potatoes, and historically supported storage, trade and provisioning across environments where harvest failure could otherwise be catastrophic. It is effectively freeze-drying without a vacuum chamber: local climate, repeated phase changes and labour substitute for industrial equipment.

## 12. Puzzle — Conway’s soldiers

An infinite checkerboard is divided by a horizontal line. Every square on or below the line may initially contain a peg; every square above it is empty.

A legal move jumps one peg horizontally or vertically over an adjacent peg into an empty square, removing the jumped peg—as in peg solitaire.

With an unlimited initial army, can you get one peg to the **fifth row** above the line using a finite sequence of moves?

The interesting part is not finding a large enough construction. It is deciding whether “unlimited resources” defeats every finite barrier—or whether there is a conserved or monotonically decreasing quantity that makes the fifth row impossible.
