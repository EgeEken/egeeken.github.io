## 2. CS and AI developments

### A weather benchmark where restoration quality and perception quality come apart

Adverse-weather datasets usually leave two variables entangled: scene content and weather severity. **FogDrive** instead renders each of 660 CARLA scenes in four matched versions—clean, then fog calibrated to 160 m, 100 m and 50 m visibility—across four cameras, LiDAR, semantic LiDAR and radar. The roughly 133,000 annotated frames therefore support controlled comparisons in which the objects and geometry stay fixed while the sensor corruption changes.

The useful result is methodological. Training on mixed fog densities improved 3D box geometry without simply scaling the dataset, while camera-restoration scores such as PSNR and SSIM were poor predictors of downstream detection. That is another warning against optimizing an intermediate representation for human-visible cleanliness when the actual objective is calibrated perception. The limitation is equally clear: CARLA gives controlled counterfactuals, not the full sensor artefacts, traffic distributions and weather interactions of real roads. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.22698)

### Treating host memory as the model store, not merely an overflow buffer

A 105-billion-parameter video diffusion transformer needs more than a terabyte of persistent weights, master weights and optimizer state. **MegaSlide-DiT** demonstrates full-parameter adaptation on one H200 by keeping that persistent state in **1.5 TB of host RAM** and streaming only transient shards to the GPU. It simultaneously replaces quadratic global attention with a motion-adaptive local operator, 3D Deformable Slide Attention, whose memory and compute grow linearly with sequence length.

The interesting systems claim is not “one GPU replaces a cluster.” PCIe or NVLink bandwidth becomes part of the training schedule, and the method adapts a pretrained model rather than training 105B parameters from scratch. The broader design pattern is still useful: separate *ownership* of model state from *execution* of the current layer, then co-design attention locality so activations do not become the next bottleneck after parameters are offloaded. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.22696)

## 3. Technical idea: wavelet trees

Suppose a sequence uses a large alphabet and you need three queries:

- `access(i)`: which symbol is at position `i`?
- `rank(c, i)`: how many copies of symbol `c` occur up to `i`?
- `select(c, k)`: where is the `k`-th copy of `c`?

A **wavelet tree** recursively divides the alphabet in half. At the root it stores one bit per sequence element: `0` if the symbol belongs to the lower half, `1` if it belongs to the upper half. The zero-subsequence and one-subsequence are then partitioned again in the children. Each node’s bitvector is augmented with constant-time binary `rank` and `select`.

To answer `rank(c, i)`, follow the root-to-leaf path for `c`. At each level, replace `i` with the number of relevant bits seen so far; that maps a position in the parent sequence into the corresponding child sequence. `access` follows the bit found at the current position, while `select` walks the path in reverse. A balanced tree gives `O(log σ)` time for alphabet size `σ`, and compressed bitvectors can bring space close to the sequence’s empirical entropy.

The same representation also answers range questions such as “what is the median value in positions `l…r`?” without materializing or sorting the subarray. This is why wavelet trees appear in compressed text indexes, genomic search and succinct analytical structures: they store a sequence while acting as an index over both positions and values. [Recent historical survey](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/entities/document/10.4230/OASIcs.Grossi.15) · [Practical query work](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.09239)

## 4. Non-CS science: cells can pull a growth factor out of its molecular restraint

Conventional wound materials either release a manufactured growth factor passively or wait for an external trigger such as light, pH or a magnetic field. A new *Nature Materials* paper uses **traction-force-activated payloads**: inhibitory aptamers fixed to a scaffold at one end and attached to a cell-adhesive RGD handle at the other. A migrating or adhering cell pulls on the handle, unfolds the construct and releases the previously bound growth factor.

The scaffold can first harvest endogenous growth factors from cells, an injury site or blood lysate, hold them in an inhibited state, then let nearby cells mechanically reactivate them. The authors report experiments in rat femur and mouse skin in vivo and human skin ex vivo, at doses orders of magnitude below current recombinant-protein standards. The design therefore makes the repairing tissue supply both the drug source and the release trigger.

This is preclinical, and wound mechanics, nuclease exposure and patient-to-patient variation will matter. But the control logic is notable: delivery is coupled to local cell adhesion and migration rather than elapsed time. In principle, that can reduce background signalling and place biological activity where cells are already trying to remodel tissue. [Paper](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41563-026-02682-8)

### Washington is hosting two partners, but bargaining with two different constraint systems

Donald Trump is meeting Volodymyr Zelenskiy and Benjamin Netanyahu in Washington today. Zelenskiy’s stated priority is anti-ballistic defence: permission to purchase Patriot interceptors, progress on licensed production and completion of a US–Ukraine drone arrangement. Netanyahu arrives with Iran, Lebanon, Saudi normalization and his October election in the background, amid a relationship with Trump that Reuters describes as increasingly strained.

The shared calendar can create an image of one presidential command centre allocating security guarantees across theatres. In practice, the bargaining mechanisms are different. Ukraine’s immediate constraint is a physical stock-and-production problem filtered through Pentagon execution and congressional appropriations. Israel’s negotiation is more political and diplomatic: how far US support extends, how another Iran pause is converted into terms, and whether Saudi normalization can be linked to nuclear cooperation without a Palestinian track.

This is an inference from the reported agendas, but it matters: personal access to the same patron does not create a common settlement mechanism. A White House meeting can change permissions and political signals quickly; it cannot by itself manufacture interceptors, reconcile coalition objectives or make regional counterparts accept the same package. ### France can host compute while importing much of the value chain

A Rexecode study reviewed by *Le Monde* projects up to **€210 billion** in French data-centre investment by 2035, but estimates that only about **30% of direct added value** would remain in France because much of the equipment is imported from the United States and Asia. RTE projections cited in the report put data-centre electricity demand at **23–28 TWh by 2035**, while a 100 MW operating site creates only about 50 direct skilled jobs.

That produces an unusual industrial-policy problem. The locally immobile inputs—land, grid capacity, cooling water, construction and political consent—sit in France. The most valuable mobile layers—accelerators, cloud platforms, model services and sometimes ownership—may remain foreign. Calling the resulting installation “sovereign” therefore depends on contracts and control: who owns the machines, who receives priority access, which jurisdiction governs the data, whether workloads can migrate, and whether French or European firms capture the service margin.

The case for building capacity is real; models and firms cannot operate at scale on declarations. But site acceleration without ownership, demand management and domestic downstream use risks socializing the infrastructure constraints while externalizing a large share of the rents. [Le Monde](https://www.lemonde.fr/en/economy/article/2026/07/27/france-strives-to-keep-up-in-global-data-center-race-as-opposition-mounts_6755896_19.html)

## 6. Signal versus noise: installed megawatts are not a sovereignty metric

The signal is that France has low-carbon electricity, engineering capacity and domestic firms such as OVHcloud, Data4 and Scaleway. Grid reservations, connection rules and heat-reuse requirements are concrete policy levers, not branding.

The noise is treating the headline capital expenditure or aggregate megawatts as equivalent to French control over AI. A foreign hyperscaler can build a French facility that imports accelerators, books profits elsewhere and reserves capacity for globally allocated workloads. Conversely, a smaller facility tied to French research, public procurement, European cloud services and enforceable access contracts may create more strategic autonomy per megawatt. The useful dashboard is therefore ownership, domestic value capture, guaranteed compute access, grid opportunity cost and downstream productive use—not the number of server halls announced.
