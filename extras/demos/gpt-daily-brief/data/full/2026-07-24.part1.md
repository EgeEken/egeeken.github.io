## 2. CS and AI developments

### Making a model depend on its own visual scratchpad

Unified multimodal models can generate intermediate images while reasoning, but that does not prove they actually use those images. A model may emit an attractive “thinking image” and still answer directly from the original inputs or its language-state shortcuts.

**View Dropout (VDrop)** attacks that causal-identification problem during training. Parts of one input view are hidden from the answer tokens while remaining visible to the tokens that generate the intermediate image. The answer therefore cannot recover all necessary geometry unless information passes through the visual scratchpad. The authors compare top-down, panoramic and point-matching intermediate renderings; panoramic visual thinking combined with VDrop is the only tested configuration they report as both learnable and informative, and it generalizes best from synthetic training scenes to five real-world out-of-distribution benchmarks.

The broader lesson is methodological. When evaluating chain-of-thought-like intermediates—textual, visual or latent—the key question is not whether the trace looks plausible but whether intervening on the trace changes the answer. VDrop is a training-time version of that intervention. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.27310)

### Video generators as perception backbones

**GenCeption** starts from a pretrained text-to-video diffusion backbone and turns it into a feed-forward, text-steered perception model. Instead of training a separate architecture for depth, surface normals, camera pose, segmentation or 3D keypoints, the system reuses the spatiotemporal and geometric priors learned while predicting video.

The authors report state-of-the-art or competitive results across those tasks, plus unusually strong data efficiency: comparable performance to some leading specialist systems with between 7 and 500 times less task-specific training data, depending on the comparison. A model trained only on synthetic human videos also transfers to real footage and to animals and robots.

The important claim is not merely that generative pretraining gives useful features; that is already familiar. It is that large-scale video generation may provide a more general visual pretraining objective than masked reconstruction or representation learning because successful generation forces the model to internalize appearance, motion, viewpoint and physical continuity simultaneously. The open question is whether this advantage survives when compute, backbone size and pretraining data are controlled rigorously. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.09024) · [Project](https://genception.github.io/)

## 3. Technical idea: zippers for immutable data structures

A **zipper** represents a location inside an immutable tree as two pieces:

1. the focused subtree; and
2. a context containing exactly the information needed to reconstruct the path back to the root.

For a binary tree, descending left stores the parent value and the untouched right subtree in the context. Moving back up combines the current focus with that stored context. Moving to a neighbouring location, inspecting it and replacing the focused node can therefore be constant-time local operations; returning the fully rebuilt root costs time proportional to the depth traversed.

This is the functional analogue of a mutable cursor or parent pointer, but without mutating the original tree. It is useful in syntax-tree transformations, structured editors, immutable DOM-like structures and interpreters where an algorithm repeatedly edits near a current location. The conceptual move is general: take a recursive data structure, differentiate it into a value plus its one-hole context, and make the “hole” explicit. [Gérard Huet, “The Zipper”](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-functional-programming/article/zipper/0C058890B8A9B588F26E6D68CF0CE204)

## 4. Non-CS science: heat can focus into crystal-directed rays at room temperature

In ordinary macroscopic intuition, heat diffuses smoothly from hot to cold. At sufficiently small scales and long phonon mean free paths, however, lattice vibrations can propagate anisotropically enough to produce **phonon focusing**: energy concentrates along preferred directions determined by the crystal’s dispersion relation.

A new *Nature Physics* paper reports room-temperature phonon focusing in cubic boron arsenide. Using nanoscale temperature mapping, the researchers observed ray-like heat patterns whose symmetry changed with crystal orientation. First-principles Boltzmann-transport simulations reproduced the patterns and attributed them to the redistribution of non-equilibrium phonons with long propagation lengths.

Phonon focusing had previously been experimentally associated mainly with cryogenic conditions. Showing it at room temperature makes directional heat routing less purely academic: in principle, crystal orientation could become a design variable for nanoscale thermal management and phonon–carrier control. This is not a ready-made “heat wire”; defects, interfaces and fabrication constraints still determine whether the effect survives in devices. [Paper](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41567-026-03335-y)

## 5. Geopolitics and political economy

### Turkey: a parliamentary bloc can migrate faster than a party organization

Özgür Özel and 91 lawmakers applied today to establish the **New Party** after leaving the CHP, following the court decision that annulled the CHP’s 2023 congress and restored Kemal Kılıçdaroğlu to its leadership. On the parliamentary arithmetic reported by Reuters, the new formation would immediately become the second-largest bloc, with 92 seats.

That makes the institutional asymmetry unusually clear. Parliamentary representation is portable: deputies can resign and reassemble almost overnight. A mass party is not. The CHP name, local branches, municipal networks, membership lists, candidate-selection machinery, property and accumulated voter habit remain contested assets. The new party’s first problem is therefore not writing a programme but converting a legislative migration into organizational continuity outside parliament.

The split may also create two opposite effects at once. It gives Özel’s camp a legally distinct vehicle that is harder to control through the specific CHP congress dispute, while introducing a coordination problem among opposition voters and office-holders. Whether the move strengthens or fragments the opposition will depend less on the initial seat count than on which organization inherits municipalities, activists and the expectation of being the focal challenger. [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/beleagured-turkish-opposition-attempts-regroup-with-new-party-2026-07-24/)

### EU sanctions are moving outward into the circumvention network

The EU’s 21st Russia sanctions package lists 48 individuals and 170 entities, bans port access for 41 additional shadow-fleet vessels, adds transaction bans on 33 Russian financial institutions and four non-EU banks, and targets 14 crypto-service platforms outside the EU. It also creates notification and possible restriction mechanisms around sales of LNG tankers.

The direction of travel matters. Early sanctions concentrated on Russian firms, banks and exports. Later rounds increasingly target the **infrastructure of evasion**: ships under third-country flags, external banks, trading intermediaries and crypto platforms. This is network enforcement rather than purely territorial enforcement.

That can raise the cost of circumvention without sealing it. Every added intermediary becomes a potential enforcement node, but also a diplomatic dispute with the jurisdiction hosting it. The package therefore expands the sanctions perimeter while increasing dependence on intelligence quality, beneficial-ownership tracing and cooperation from states that are not politically committed to the EU’s war aims. [Council of the EU](https://www.consilium.europa.eu/en/policies/sanctions-against-russia/timeline-packages-sanctions-since-february-2022/)

## 6. Signal versus noise: France’s PMI improved to almost no contraction

France’s flash composite PMI rose from **47.2 in June to 49.6 in July**. Services improved from 46.8 to 49.8, while manufacturing output eased from 51.2 to exactly 50.0.

The signal is that the pace of contraction slowed sharply and business conditions are near stabilization. The noise would be describing this as a recovery. A diffusion index below 50 still indicates that more firms report declining activity than increasing activity, and 49.6 is statistically and economically close to stagnation. The composition is also mixed: services nearly stopped contracting just as manufacturing lost its modest expansion.

So the disciplined reading is: **less bad, with a plausible stabilization signal, but no demonstrated growth trend yet**. One or two subsequent months above 50, preferably accompanied by improving new orders and employment, would be stronger evidence. [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/business/french-private-sector-contraction-eased-july-flash-pmis-show-2026-07-24/)
