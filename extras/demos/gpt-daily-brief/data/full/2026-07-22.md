## 2. CS / AI developments

- **Masked Visual Actions turns action prediction into partial video completion.** Instead of encoding a robot action as a discrete command or joint vector, the system reveals selected future pixels—such as the gripper trajectory—and asks a video model to complete the rest. The same representation supports forward prediction, inverse action inference, planning and policy evaluation, and can transfer across embodiments because the interface remains visual. The useful idea is not merely “video models for robotics”: action is represented in the model’s native output space, reducing the translation boundary between perception and control. [Project](https://masked-visual-actions.github.io/) · [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.19343)
- **Appearance Pointers make regional visual references first-class tokens.** A pointer binds an image region or mask to a compact token that can be reused in a prompt, allowing a multimodal model to reason about several objects or regions without repeatedly re-encoding cropped images or relying on ambiguous textual descriptions. The method is designed to add region correspondence and aggregation without retraining the base model. Architecturally, this resembles a typed reference in a programming language: the prompt can pass an object identity around rather than redescribing it each time. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.19344)

## 3. Technical / research idea — Asymmetric Numeral Systems

**Asymmetric Numeral Systems (ANS)** is an entropy-coding family that reaches arithmetic-coding-like compression while operating through a single integer state and table or arithmetic transitions. Huffman coding assigns an integer number of bits to each symbol; arithmetic coding approaches fractional average lengths but traditionally maintains an interval. ANS instead makes the *state distribution* carry the probability model: frequent symbols occupy more states, so encoding them expands the state by fewer bits on average.

The practical form, **rANS**, alternates a reversible state update with renormalization: whenever the state grows beyond a range, low bits are emitted; decoding performs the inverse transition and pulls bits back in. That makes the coder naturally stack-like—symbols are decoded in reverse encoding order—which is often convenient in codecs and learned-compression pipelines. Its appeal is the engineering compromise: near-optimal entropy coding, compact state, fast table-based implementations, and easy interleaving for instruction-level parallelism. [Jarek Duda’s paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/1311.2540)

## 6. Signal vs noise

**Noise:** reading an unexpectedly high low-energy fusion yield as evidence that practical “cold fusion” has arrived.

**Signal:** experiments with deuterium-loaded palladium and titanium report fusion yields at sub-keV energies far above a naïve unscreened extrapolation, with a plateau below about 2 keV. That is a real nuclear-physics anomaly worth explaining through electron screening, lattice effects and non-equilibrium deuteron populations. It is not a power source: enhanced reaction probability at tiny beam energies says nothing by itself about net energy after target preparation, beam generation, heat losses and extremely low absolute event rates. [Nature Communications](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-026-74421-1)

## 7. Marxist fragment

> “The realm of freedom actually begins only where labour which is determined by necessity and mundane considerations ceases.”

— Karl Marx, *Capital*, Volume III, Chapter 48, translated by David Fernbach. [Primary text](https://www.marxists.org/archive/marx/works/1894-c3/ch48.htm)

Marx is not proposing that freedom consists in choosing a nicer job under the same constraint. Necessary production remains, but freedom begins when associated producers consciously regulate it, reduce the working day, and place it under collective control rather than allowing market compulsion to appear as an external natural law. The passage therefore joins institutional control and temporal liberation: democratic planning without reduced necessary labour is incomplete, while leisure financed by domination elsewhere is not universal freedom.

## 8. Two philosophy / political fragments

### Adam Smith — the planner’s model omits agency

> “every single piece has a principle of motion of its own”

— Adam Smith, *The Theory of Moral Sentiments*, Part VI, Section II. [Primary text](https://www.marxists.org/reference/archive/smith-adam/works/moral/part06/part6b.htm)

Smith’s “man of system” imagines society as pieces on a chessboard, but people do not move only according to administrative design. The conservative implication is a warning against overconfident planning; the broader systems lesson is sharper: any institution that models agents as passive components will encounter adaptation, evasion and counter-strategy.

### Benjamin Constant — modern liberty is protected private agency

> “Individual independence is the first need of the moderns”

— Benjamin Constant, “The Liberty of Ancients Compared with that of Moderns” (1819). [English text](https://www.davidmhart.com/liberty/FrenchClassicalLiberals/Constant/1819-LiberteAnciens/EnglishVersion.html)

Constant distinguishes ancient liberty—direct collective sovereignty—from modern liberty—security of private life, speech, property and association. His argument is useful even from a socialist perspective because it identifies a real failure mode: collective political power can reproduce domination if participation is treated as compensation for weak individual protections.

## 9. French note — *tu m’étonnes*

In informal French, **tu m’étonnes** often means the opposite of its literal wording. Rather than “you surprise me,” it commonly means **“no kidding,” “of course,” or “tell me about it.”**

- *Il était crevé après douze heures de boulot. — Tu m’étonnes.*  
  “He was exhausted after twelve hours of work. — No kidding.”
- Tone matters. With genuine rising surprise, it can retain the literal meaning; with a flat or emphatic delivery, it signals that the conclusion is obvious. [Usage note](https://www.lawlessfrench.com/expressions/tu-metonnes/)

## 11. Unrelated fresh fact — why old blue paint can turn grey-brown

**Smalt** was a common blue pigment made by grinding cobalt-coloured potassium glass. European painters used it widely from the fifteenth through eighteenth centuries because it was cheaper and more available than ultramarine.

Its blue is not chemically permanent inside oil paint. Potassium can leach from the glass particles into the binder, changing the cobalt’s local coordination and therefore its light absorption; the altered oil can also yellow. A layer originally painted as a cool blue can consequently become pale, grey or brown. Conservation science therefore does more than identify ingredients: it reconstructs an artwork’s lost colour relationships by modelling pigment–binder reactions over centuries. [National Gallery technical study](https://www.nationalgallery.org.uk/research/publications/technical-bulletin/investigation-of-pigment-medium-interaction-processes-in-oil-paint-containing-degraded-smalt)

## 12. Puzzle — Braess’s paradox

Four thousand drivers travel from **Start** to **End**. They can use either route:

- Start → A takes **x/100 minutes**, where *x* is the number of drivers on that road; A → End takes **45 minutes**.
- Start → B takes **45 minutes**; B → End takes **y/100 minutes**, where *y* is the number of drivers on that road.

With no road between A and B, traffic splits evenly and every driver takes **65 minutes**.

Now the city adds a zero-minute road from **A to B**. Every driver independently chooses the fastest route available. At equilibrium, all drivers choose Start → A → B → End and everyone takes **80 minutes**.

**Why can adding a free road make every selfishly optimizing driver worse off?** What property would a routing or pricing mechanism need in order to recover the 65-minute outcome? [Background](https://arxiv.org/abs/2009.02158)
