## 7. Marxist fragment

> “the direct relationship of the owners of the conditions of production to the direct producers … reveals the innermost secret”

— Karl Marx, *Capital*, Volume III, chapter 47. [Primary text](https://www.marxists.org/archive/marx/works/1894-c3/ch47.htm)

Marx’s point is more specific than “economics influences politics.” The form in which surplus labour is extracted—through slavery, feudal rent, wage labour or another relation—structures the corresponding forms of dependence, sovereignty and state power. Legal and political institutions are not mechanically deduced from an economy, but neither are they free-floating arrangements chosen independently of property and production relations.

Applied to contemporary party crises, this cautions against treating constitutional form or parliamentary arithmetic as the whole political structure. The durable distribution of workplaces, property, media, municipal patronage and organizational resources constrains which formal political realignments can become socially effective.

## 8. Two philosophy and political fragments

### Thomas Paine on normalized error

> “a long habit of not thinking a thing WRONG, gives it a superficial appearance of being RIGHT”

— Thomas Paine, *Common Sense*. [Primary text](https://gutenberg.org/files/3755/3755-h/3755-h.htm)

Paine identifies a conservative mechanism that is weaker than argument but often more durable: repetition converts contingency into apparent necessity. An institution need not justify itself continuously if alternatives have become difficult to imagine. The useful question is therefore not only “is this rule defensible?” but “which part of its authority comes from having survived long enough to look natural?”

### Étienne de La Boétie on withdrawn support

> “Soiés resolus de ne seruir plus; et vous voilà libres.”

— Étienne de La Boétie, *Discours de la servitude volontaire*. [Primary text](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/62051/62051-h/62051-h.htm)

La Boétie’s provocation is that domination depends on distributed acts of compliance, not only force concentrated at the top. Its weakness is the inverse of its insight: support is not equally voluntary when livelihoods, repression and coordination failures make individual withdrawal costly. Read against Marx rather than instead of him, the passage becomes a question about organization: under what material conditions can scattered refusals become collective power rather than isolated sacrifice?

## 9. French usage: “ça se tente”

**Ça se tente** means roughly **“it is worth a try,” “we could give it a shot,”** or **“why not try it?”** It presents an option as uncertain but sufficiently plausible and low-risk to attempt.

- *Le concert commence dans une heure, mais il reste peut-être des places. Ça se tente.*
- *On peut finir le prototype ce soir ? — Ça se tente, mais il faudra simplifier deux features.*

It is informal and usually more lightly optimistic than *ça vaut le coup*, which more directly claims that the expected benefit justifies the effort. Do not confuse it with *ça te tente ?*, which asks whether something appeals to the listener. [Usage examples](https://context.reverso.net/translation/french-english/%C3%A7a%2Bse%2Btente)

## 11. Unrelated fact: preserving the *Vasa* required replacing water inside wood

The Swedish warship *Vasa* sank in Stockholm harbour on its maiden voyage in 1628 and was raised largely intact in 1961. Its oak had survived because it remained waterlogged in cold, brackish conditions. Once exposed to air, ordinary drying would have removed the water supporting the wood’s degraded cellular structure, causing severe shrinkage, cracking and collapse.

Conservators therefore sprayed the ship for **17 years** with polyethylene glycol, a water-soluble polymer that gradually displaced water inside the wood and provided support as it dried; controlled drying then continued for another nine years. The treatment saved the hull but did not end conservation. Iron and sulfur compounds inside the timber can drive acid-forming reactions, and the museum still controls humidity, temperature and structural support as a continuous engineering problem rather than treating preservation as a completed restoration. ## 12. Puzzle: Sleeping Beauty and self-locating probability

A fair coin is tossed on Sunday while Sleeping Beauty is asleep.

- If it lands **heads**, she is awakened on Monday, interviewed and put back to sleep.
- If it lands **tails**, she is awakened on Monday and Tuesday, interviewed each time, and her memory of the earlier awakening is erased.
- Whenever she wakes, she cannot tell which day it is or whether she has previously been awakened.

At an awakening, Beauty is asked:

> **What probability should you assign to the coin having landed heads?**

The two standard answers are **1/2** and **1/3**. Do not merely choose one. State what the probability is conditional on, identify the relevant sample space, and explain whether learning “I am awake now” supplies new evidence or only self-locating information.
