## 2. CS / AI developments

- **Computer vision as one generative interface.** *SenseNova-Vision* expresses detection, OCR, keypoints, segmentation, depth, normals, point maps and camera pose through one multimodal model: symbolic answers are emitted as text, dense predictions as images, and mixed tasks as both. The architectural point is not merely “one model does many benchmarks”; it is that task identity, region selection and decoding convention move into the prompt/API layer, with no dedicated prediction heads. That could make capability composition much easier. The caveat is that removing task-specific heads does not remove task-specific semantics: metric calibration, coordinate conventions and error tolerances still have to live somewhere, and a generative decoder may be a poor place to demand exact geometry. [Paper](https://huggingface.co/papers/2607.06560)
- **Medical multimodal data engineering may matter more than another encoder.** *MedPMC* processed 6.1 million permissively licensed PubMed Central articles into **11 million** image–text pairs. Manual review found **95.3%** of sampled images medically relevant, versus **19.7%** in a prior PMC-derived dataset; an architecture-matched model then beat the strongest biomedical CLIP baseline while using fewer than half as many pairs. The interesting result is the pipeline—figure screening, panel separation, caption alignment and medical classification—not the leaderboard number. It is a fairly direct demonstration that “dataset scale” without fidelity can be mostly duplicated noise. [Paper](https://huggingface.co/papers/2607.07673)

## 3. Technical / research idea — equality saturation

Compiler rewrites have a **phase-ordering problem**: applying one locally sensible optimization can hide the pattern needed by another. An **e-graph** avoids committing early. It compactly stores many equivalent expressions in equivalence classes; rewrite rules add alternatives rather than replacing the current expression. After exploration, an extractor chooses the cheapest representative under a cost model.

For example, distributivity, associativity, constant folding and domain-specific tensor rewrites can all coexist in the graph. The optimizer does not need to guess whether to expand or factor first. This is useful in compilers, hardware synthesis and query/tensor optimization, where the profitable form depends on later context. The hard part simply moves: unrestricted saturation can explode, and a bad cost model will confidently extract the wrong program. The practical craft is therefore bounded rewrite scheduling plus a cost function that reflects the actual backend. [POPL 2021: `egg`](https://popl21.sigplan.org/details/POPL-2021-research-papers/23/egg-Fast-and-Extensible-Equality-Saturation)

## 4. Non-CS science

- **Cells appear to transmit gravity into mitochondrial protein synthesis through adhesion mechanics.** Researchers used ribosome profiling on human HEK293 cells cultured on the ISS, *C. elegans* flown in space, simulated microgravity and unloaded mouse hindlimbs. Microgravity reduced translation of mitochondrially encoded proteins without an equivalent fall in their RNA, pointing to translational control rather than simple transcriptional shutdown.
- The proposed chain is unusually concrete: weaker laminin–integrin adhesion reduces FAK/RAC1/PAK1 signalling, alters BAD/Bcl-2 activity and mitochondrial fatty-acid synthesis, changes malonyl-CoA availability and thereby the malonylation state of the mitochondrial translation machinery. The mouse unloading result matters because it connects spaceflight biology to ordinary disuse and immobilization. It is still a mechanistic study in model systems, not a demonstrated therapy for muscle loss. [Nature Communications](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-026-74493-z)

Several hundred U.S. troops were quietly withdrawn from Estonia, leaving fewer than 100 there compared with roughly six times that number last winter. Washington also cancelled a planned movement of about 5,000 troops to Poland; roughly 1,000 U.S. troops reportedly left Lithuania in June without replacement. Meanwhile, summit projects such as a multinational defence bank and long-range missile consortium will take years to deliver. [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/business/aerospace-defense/quiet-us-pullback-eastern-europe-sparks-wider-allied-worries-2026-07-16/)

A forward deployment is not just a troop count. It is a **tripwire**, a standing command-and-logistics network, and a reduction in reinforcement latency. European procurement can eventually replace capability, but it cannot instantly replace the political certainty created when U.S. personnel would be involved from the first hour of a conflict. The likely response is more European coordination and bilateral hedging; the danger is that emergency procurement deepens dependence on U.S. systems precisely while confidence in U.S. presence declines.

## 7. Marxist fragment

> “The instrument of labour, when it takes the form of a machine, immediately becomes a competitor of the workman himself.”

— Karl Marx, *Capital*, Volume I, Chapter 15, Moore–Aveling translation. [Primary text](https://www.marxists.org/archive/marx/works/1867-c1/ch15.htm)

This is not a claim that machinery is intrinsically anti-worker. Marx’s mechanism is specific: under capitalism, the worker must sell labour-power, while the machine is introduced as capital’s property and bargaining weapon. Productivity gains can therefore appear to the worker as redundancy, wage pressure and strike-breaking rather than shorter labour time. Applied to AI, the relevant question is not whether a model “replaces tasks” in the abstract, but who owns the system, who captures the productivity gain, and whether displaced labour retains a claim on the resulting output.

## 8. Two philosophy / political fragments

### Spinoza — right and effective power

> “Her right is co-extensive with her power.”

— Spinoza, *Theological-Political Treatise*, Chapter XVI, R. H. M. Elwes translation. [Primary text](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/992/992-h/992-h.htm)

Spinoza’s natural-right vocabulary is deliberately descriptive: before a common authority exists, a right extends only as far as an agent can actually act. This is a useful corrective to international-law rhetoric that treats unenforced declarations as settled reality. But it is not a theory of legitimacy: the fact that power determines what can be done does not establish what ought to be accepted.

### Tocqueville — majority rule can remain arbitrary

> “This irresistible authority is a constant fact, and its judicious exercise is an accidental occurrence.”

— Alexis de Tocqueville, *Democracy in America*, Part I, section on the tyranny of the majority, Henry Reeve translation. [Primary text](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/815/815-h/815-h.htm)

Tocqueville’s target is not elections but unconstrained social and institutional concentration. A majority can act through perfectly legal procedures while making dissent professionally and socially costly. His strongest point is therefore institutional rather than moral: popular authorization does not by itself create error correction, minority protection or independent centres capable of resisting a durable consensus.

## 9. French note — *histoire de + infinitif*

**Histoire de** introduces a light, practical or slightly pretextual purpose: “just to,” “so as to,” “for the sake of.”

- *Je vais partir un peu tôt, histoire d’éviter la foule.* — I’ll leave a little early, just to avoid the crowd.
- *On prend un café, histoire de faire le point ?* — Shall we get coffee, just to catch up / take stock?

It is more conversational and less solemn than *afin de*. The implied purpose is often modest rather than the formal objective of the whole action.

## 11. Unrelated fresh fact — wooden receipts that authenticated themselves

The English Exchequer recorded debts with **tally sticks**: notches encoded the amount, then the stick was split lengthwise. The payer received the longer “stock”; officials kept the shorter “foil.” At audit, the pieces had to fit and the notches had to align. [London Museum](https://www.londonmuseum.org.uk/collections/v/object-71971/tally-stick/)

The split followed the wood’s irregular grain, producing a physical matching problem that was difficult to counterfeit with pre-industrial tools—roughly a material authentication token rather than a written receipt. Tallies remained in use until 1826. Their disposal then produced an absurd historical coda: in 1834, workers burned two cartloads in underfloor stoves at the Palace of Westminster, overheated the structure and helped cause the fire that destroyed most of the old Parliament buildings. [UK Parliament](https://www.parliament.uk/about/living-heritage/building/palace/architecture/palacestructure/great-fire/)

## 12. Puzzle / dilemma — Death in Damascus

Death is an exceptionally reliable predictor. Weeks ago, Death wrote in a sealed appointment book where you would be at midnight tonight: **Damascus** or **Aleppo**. You die if you are in the predicted city.

You are currently in Damascus. If you stay, that is strong evidence Death predicted Damascus. If you flee to Aleppo, it is equally strong evidence Death predicted Aleppo. Yet the book is already fixed, so causal reasoning seems to say: whatever you were about to do, switch.

**What is the rational policy?** Does a genuinely unpredictable coin flip solve the problem, or does choosing to randomize merely become another predictable action? What assumption about prediction, causation or evidence is doing the real work?
