The unresolved contradiction is institutional. Complex production requires durable expertise and enforcement, yet durable administrative organisations can regenerate hierarchy and autonomous interests. Lenin’s answer is radical democratisation plus simplified administration, but the later Soviet state demonstrated how quickly emergency coercion, technical dependence and party monopoly could overwhelm the recallability side of that formula. The passage is therefore stronger as a statement of the problem than as evidence that the problem was solved.

## 8. Two philosophy and political fragments

### Henry David Thoreau: legality does not discharge personal judgment

> “It is not desirable to cultivate a respect for the law, so much as for the right.”

— Henry David Thoreau, *Resistance to Civil Government* / *Civil Disobedience*, 1849. [Primary text](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/71/71-h/71-h.htm)

Thoreau wrote against slavery and the Mexican–American War, arguing that participation in an unjust state cannot be excused merely because its procedures are legal. The force of the claim is its refusal of moral outsourcing. Its weakness is institutional: conflicting consciences do not themselves supply a common rule, and a politics composed only of individual refusal cannot reliably distinguish principled resistance from private domination.

### Epicurus: justice as a revisable agreement against harm

> “There never was an absolute justice, but only an agreement made in reciprocal association … providing against the infliction or suffering of harm.”

— Epicurus, *Principal Doctrines*, doctrine 33, translated by Robert Drew Hicks. [Primary text](https://classics.mit.edu/Epicurus/princdoc.html)

This is not the claim that justice is whatever rulers declare. For Epicurus, a rule is just insofar as it serves mutual security; when circumstances change and a rule no longer provides reciprocal advantage, its claim to justice changes too. That offers a material test for institutions, but creates friction with universal-rights theories: if justice depends entirely on reciprocal benefit, protections for people with little bargaining power may become contingent on what stronger parties perceive as useful.

## 9. French usage: **je suis preneur / preneuse**

**Être preneur de quelque chose** means that you are interested, receptive or happy to receive it—not necessarily that you are literally buying it.

- *Si tu as des références sur le sujet, je suis preneur.*
- *Je suis preneuse de retours, même critiques.*
- *À ce prix-là, je suis preneur* keeps the older commercial sense: “I’ll take it.”

It is common in conversation and ordinary workplace French. With a specific proposal, **je suis partant(e)** is often more active: *Je suis partant pour le concert.* In a formal email, *je serais intéressé(e) par vos suggestions* is smoother than repeatedly using *je suis preneur*. The noun agrees with the speaker: **preneur** / **preneuse**. ## 11. Unrelated fact: a whale’s earplug can preserve a chemical timeline of its entire life

Baleen whales accumulate dense wax plugs in their ear canals. Alternating light and dark **laminae** form at roughly six-month intervals, so researchers can age the animal by counting layers and chemically sample successive periods rather than obtaining only one lifetime-average measurement.

In a 2013 study, researchers sectioned the 24 layers of a 25.4-centimetre earplug from a 12-year-old male blue whale. They reconstructed cortisol, testosterone, persistent organic pollutants and mercury at six-month resolution. The record indicated sexual maturity at roughly ten years, substantial maternal transfer of several organic contaminants during the first year, and distinct mercury pulses.

The method matters because blubber can show how much of a lipophilic contaminant is present but not **when** exposure occurred. Earplug layers add chronology, turning museum specimens into historical archives of development, pollution and stress. Later work has extended the approach across whales and longer historical periods, though interpretation still requires care because hormones reflect multiple interacting stressors. ## 12. Puzzle: Protagoras and Euathlus

Protagoras agrees to teach Euathlus law. Their contract says Euathlus will pay the tuition **after he wins his first court case**.

Euathlus finishes his training but does not practise, so he never wins a case. Protagoras sues him for payment.

- **Protagoras:** If I win this lawsuit, Euathlus must pay by the court’s judgment. If I lose, Euathlus has won his first case and must pay under the contract.
- **Euathlus:** If I win, the court has ruled that I need not pay. If I lose, I still have not won a case, so the contract has not triggered.

What should the court decide? More precisely, at what moment should it evaluate the contractual condition: before this judgment, immediately after it, or only in a later action?
