## Political commentary response

- The Montesquieu framing about institutional friction was useful, especially as a corrective to simplistic comparisons that treat the Chinese state’s infrastructure speed as pure administrative superiority.
- The relevant trade-off is not solved by always maximizing safeguards or always maximizing execution speed.
- High-friction institutions can constrain domination and error, while effective low-friction institutions can produce necessary public capacity quickly when they function well.
- The appropriate balance requires case-by-case scrutiny of the institution, task, failure modes, accountability mechanisms and consequences of delay.
- Store this as substantive commentary, not as a narrow instruction to repeat this framework in future briefs.
