## Moral-luck dilemma response

The user accepts harsher punishment for the driver whose reckless act actually kills someone, but not because punishment objectively measures abstract wrongdoing or evil. Their account is consequentialist and institutional:

- punishment is primarily meant to deter conduct;
- the knowledge that a harmful outcome can trigger severe punishment is part of the deterrent mechanism;
- luck already determines real-world consequences;
- legal punishment cannot be perfectly objective without an omniscient legal system;
- therefore outcome-sensitive punishment is not anomalous within the actual function and epistemic limits of law.

Store this as a substantive response, not as a narrow constraint on future dilemmas.
