### section.philosophy_fragments
I generally agree with the root claim of the calculation problem which is that it is impossible to simulate and calculate the exact inner workings of a market economy, but the reason why i remain in support of planned economies is we don't have to do that at all, because the goal of an economic system is to produce and distribute according to needs as effectively and efficiently as possible. The market is just as much of an "estimator" for that as is any centralized or decentralized planning. The market could only be assumed to be a state of perfection if all participants were all knowing actors with perfect agency and somehow working under a unified goal of efficiency. Since that is not the case, it is simply a process that averages out into a functional system, that i would give to the market economies, they function pretty well, i just believe we can do even better. There's absolutely no reason to assume the end goal of a planned economy is to simulate market demands to its fullest extent and thus the critique that it's incapable of doing that is not a particularly strong critique.
